# A08-Green-Line-B-Weather-and-Event-Disruptions.ipynb
The analysis aims to inform MBTA officials and Boston-area commuters about how adverse weather, public events, and seasonal volume impact the travel times and reliability of the Green Line B Branch.   
MBTA Green Line B Travel Time DisruptionsOverviewThis project analyzes how adverse weather, public events, and seasonal passenger volume impact the travel times and reliability of the MBTA Green Line B Branch. By comparing transit data from September and December 2025, this analysis aims to provide actionable insights for commuters planning their trips and MBTA officials managing service disruptions.   The Green Line B Branch is unique because it operates both above ground (from Boston College to Kenmore) and below ground (to Government Center), making it highly susceptible to street-level disruptions.   Key FindingsAbove-Ground vs. Below-Ground Reliability: Above-ground segments are the primary cause of schedule unpredictability. Even on normal days without precipitation or events, above-ground segments show nearly triple the variance of underground segments (192.9s vs. 66.7s standard deviation).   Peak vs. Off-Peak Performance: While peak hours add approximately 1.5 minutes to the median end-to-end travel time, they actually result in a tighter standard deviation (5.6 minutes) compared to off-peak hours (6.6 minutes), making rush hour slightly more predictable.   Seasonal Baselines: Baseline travel is significantly slower during the September back-to-school rush, with a median of 771.5 seconds per segment, compared to 696.8 seconds in December.   Directional Variations: During the AM peak, inbound trips (Boston College to Government Center) are slower than outbound trips (46.4 minutes vs. 43.7 minutes). In contrast, outbound and inbound median travel times are roughly equal during the PM peak (50.5 minutes vs. 50.2 minutes).   Data Sources & MethodologyThe findings are derived from a unified dataset of 539,798 merged records built from three primary sources:   MBTA Rapid Transit Travel Times: Point-to-point travel time data for the Green-B line.   NOAA Local Climatological Data: Hourly weather logs (precipitation, dry bulb temperature, wind speed, snow depth) recorded at Boston Logan Airport.   Analyze Boston (City of Boston): Special event permits to quantify the impact of public gatherings near the transit line.
